Burst Pipe In West Palm Beach: What To Do In The First 10 Minutes

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Last updated October 2026.

A burst pipe is one of the few plumbing situations where the first few minutes genuinely matter. Here's exactly what to do in West Palm Beach before the plumber arrives, from the shutoff to the paperwork.

Step One: Shut Off The Water

Find your main shutoff valve and turn it fully off. This is the single most important action you can take, and it's why every household member should know where this valve is before an emergency happens, not during one. If you can't locate the main shutoff quickly, closing the nearest fixture or branch valve at least slows the flow while you keep looking.

The International Plumbing Code (IPC), which the Florida Building Code, Plumbing is based on, calls for a shutoff valve on the building's water supply. That means your home should have a main valve you can use, and finding it before an emergency is the single most useful step you can take.

Step Two: Shut Off Electricity To The Affected Area If Water Is Near Outlets Or Panels

Standing water near electrical outlets, panels, or plugged-in appliances is a genuine safety hazard. If it's safe to reach your breaker panel without walking through standing water, shut off power to the affected area before doing anything else in that space.

Step Three: Start Removing Standing Water

Towels, a wet-dry shop vacuum, or simply pushing water toward a drain all help limit how much water gets absorbed into flooring and drywall while you wait for help. Every few minutes matters here, since materials that stay wet longer sustain more damage and take longer to dry out.

Step Four: Call For Emergency Dispatch

Once the water is shut off and you've started containing what's already out, call for emergency plumbing help. Describe what happened clearly: where the pipe burst, how much water is involved, and whether it's still actively flowing despite the main shutoff (which can indicate the break is on the street side of your shutoff valve).

Step Five: Document Before You Clean Up Further

If you're planning to file an insurance claim, a few photos of the affected area before extensive cleanup begins can matter for that process. This shouldn't come at the expense of actually stopping and containing the water, but it's worth a minute once the immediate crisis is under control.

West Palm Beach Specifics: Shutoffs, Panels, And Paperwork

In many slab-on-grade Florida homes, the main shutoff sits outside near where the line enters the house, or in the garage or a utility closet. Find it before you need it. The valve at the street meter box typically belongs to the utility, so leave it for them.

Breaker panels are commonly in the garage or on an exterior wall here, which makes it easier to cut power to affected circuits. If water is reaching the panel itself, stay clear and call the utility or an electrician.

Keep the plumber's repair invoice and your photos. The City of West Palm Beach's leak-credit rule for city water customers requires a certified plumber's repair within seven days, and Public Utilities asks for the repair bill with the request.

What Happens When The Technician Arrives

Once on-site, the first priority is confirming the water is fully shut off and identifying the actual break point. It isn't always where the water is visibly coming from, because water travels along the path of least resistance before it shows up. From there, the immediate repair or a temporary stabilization is made, followed by an honest conversation about whether the affected area needs water damage restoration in addition to the plumbing fix.

Common Mistakes People Make In The Panic

Two mistakes come up often. One is running to the hardware store for a patch kit instead of shutting off the water first, which wastes time while damage continues. The other is turning the water back on early to "test" whether the leak stopped, which can cause a second wave of water. Sticking to the shutoff-first, contain-second sequence avoids both.

Why Practicing The Sequence Matters More Than Reading About It

Reading this sequence once is useful. Locating your main shutoff valve right now, before an emergency, is better. Confirm that it turns smoothly, since a valve that's seized from years of never being touched is its own problem worth catching early. A valve that's hard to turn under normal conditions will be even harder to deal with while water is actively flooding the area around it.

The Bottom Line

A burst pipe is stressful precisely because it feels like an emergency where every second counts, and in a real sense, it is. But the actual sequence of what to do is simple enough to remember under pressure once you've read it through: shut off the water, address any electrical hazard, contain what's already out, then call for help.

Everything after that point is what a professional handles. Knowing this sequence ahead of time, and knowing where your shutoff valve actually is, turns a chaotic emergency into a manageable few minutes followed by a straightforward repair.

Frequently Asked

What if I can't find my main shutoff valve during an emergency?

Closing the shutoff valve on the specific fixture or line nearest the break, if there is one, can slow the flow while you keep searching or wait for help to arrive.

Should I try to fix the pipe myself before help arrives?

No. A burst pipe under pressure isn't a safe DIY repair, and attempting one can make the situation worse, and can cause injury if the pipe is still under pressure when you attempt to touch or clamp it. Focus on shutoff and containment, and let a professional handle the actual repair.

How quickly can emergency dispatch actually reach me?

Response time depends on your specific location within our coverage area, but our emergency line is staffed 24/7 specifically for situations like this, and dispatch begins as soon as you call.

Is it normal for water to keep dripping even after the main shutoff is closed?

A small amount of residual water draining from the line above the break point is normal. Continued strong flow after shutoff usually means the break is upstream of your valve. That is important information to relay when you call so dispatch can plan accordingly before arriving, including bringing any specialized equipment the situation might call for.
