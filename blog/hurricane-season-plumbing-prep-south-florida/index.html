How To Prepare Your Plumbing For Hurricane Season

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team

Hurricane season brings more than wind and flooding risk to South Florida homes. It also puts real, predictable stress on plumbing systems, from sump pumps working overtime during heavy rain to sewer backups when municipal systems are overwhelmed. A little preparation before a storm is far more effective than reacting once one is already bearing down.

Test Your Sump Pump Now, Not During The Storm

If your property has a sump pump, this is the single most important pre-season check. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and confirm the pump activates and drains it properly. A pump that hasn't run in months can fail silently, and you won't find out until the exact moment you need it most.

Know Where Your Main Shutoff Is

Every household member should know the location of the main water shutoff valve and how to turn it, before a burst pipe or supply line failure during a storm turns into a much bigger problem while everyone is figuring out where the valve is.

Consider A Battery Backup For Your Sump Pump

Power outages are common during and after hurricanes, and a sump pump that can't run because the power is out defeats the purpose entirely. A battery backup system keeps the pump running through exactly the outage scenario most likely to cause flooding in the first place.

Check For Backflow Protection If You Have One

Homes with backflow preventers, common on properties with irrigation systems, should have those devices checked as part of seasonal prep, since a failed backflow device during heavy flooding can allow contaminated water to enter the clean supply line.

Know The Difference Between Mitigation And Restoration

If water does get into your home during a storm, the response happens in two phases: mitigation, the emergency step of stopping the water and protecting unaffected areas, followed by restoration, the drying and rebuild process. Knowing this ahead of time means you're not trying to understand the process for the first time while water is actively spreading.

Clear Your Drains Before The Season Starts

Heavy, sustained rain exposes any existing drainage weaknesses fast. A drain that's been running a little slow for months can turn into a real backup problem the first time several inches of rain fall in a short period. A pre-season drain check, particularly for any drain that's already shown signs of slow flow, is a low-cost way to avoid a much bigger headache mid-storm.

Have A Plan For If You Lose Water Service Entirely

Storms can occasionally disrupt municipal water service itself, not just your home's plumbing. Keeping a reasonable supply of stored water on hand as part of general storm prep covers this scenario, and it's worth doing regardless of how solid your home's own plumbing and pumps are, since municipal disruption is outside anyone's individual control.

After The Storm: What To Check Before Assuming Everything's Fine

Once a storm passes, run each fixture briefly and check for unusual sounds, discoloration, or reduced pressure, all of which can indicate storm-related plumbing stress even without an obvious leak. A sump pump that ran continuously for hours during the storm is also worth a follow-up check, since sustained heavy use is exactly when a marginal pump is most likely to show early signs of wear.

Building A Simple Pre-Season Checklist

Rather than trying to remember every individual step each year, it helps to write down a short checklist once and reuse it: test the sump pump, locate and confirm the main shutoff works smoothly, check any backflow device, clear slow drains, and confirm battery backup power is fresh if you have one. Running through this in under thirty minutes at the start of the season is far less stressful than trying to remember all of it while a storm is already approaching on the forecast. Keeping the checklist somewhere accessible, like taped inside a utility closet, also means anyone in the household can run through it, not just whoever normally handles home maintenance.

The Bottom Line

None of this preparation prevents a hurricane from happening, but it meaningfully changes how your home's plumbing systems perform during and after one. A sump pump that's been tested ahead of time is far more likely to actually work when it matters most than one that's simply been sitting untested for a year. The same goes for knowing your shutoff location, having backup power ready, and understanding the difference between mitigation and restoration before you're in the middle of needing either. Storm season in this part of Florida isn't optional to plan for, it happens every year, which makes this exactly the kind of preparation worth building into a genuine annual routine rather than something to think about only once a storm is already named and approaching.

Frequently Asked

How far ahead of hurricane season should I do this?

Ideally at the start of the season, but any preparation done before a storm is named and approaching is better than none. A sump pump test takes five minutes and can be done any time.

What if I don't have a sump pump but I'm worried about flooding?

If your property has a history of standing water during heavy rain, it's worth having one installed proactively rather than waiting for the first flood to make the case for you.

Does normal homeowner's insurance cover storm-related plumbing damage?

Coverage varies significantly by policy and by whether the damage is classified as flood versus wind-driven rain versus a mechanical failure, so it's worth reviewing your specific policy details before storm season rather than during an active claim.
