Pool Owners: Is It A Pool Leak Or A Plumbing Leak?

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team

Pool ownership is common across this area, and it adds a genuine wrinkle to diagnosing an unexplained rise in your water bill or a mysteriously dropping pool level. Before assuming it's one or the other, there are a few things worth checking, since the answer changes who you should call.

The Bucket Test For Your Pool

Place a bucket on the pool step so its water level matches the pool's, then mark both levels and check again after 24 hours. If the pool has dropped noticeably more than the bucket (which loses water only to evaporation), that points to a genuine pool leak rather than simple evaporation, which is normal, especially during hot, dry stretches.

Where Pool Leaks Actually Happen

The most common pool leak sources are the equipment pad plumbing (the pipes connecting the pool to the pump, filter, and heater), the pool shell itself at a crack or fitting, and buried lines running between the pool and equipment pad. Equipment pad leaks are usually the easiest to spot visually; buried line leaks are the ones that require professional detection.

Checking Your House Meter Separately

While you're investigating the pool, it's worth also checking your household water meter with everything off, including any auto-fill on the pool. If the meter is moving even with the pool's auto-fill temporarily shut off, that points to a leak in your household plumbing rather than the pool system, a completely separate problem requiring a different kind of inspection.

Why This Distinction Actually Matters

A pool leak is typically addressed by a pool service technician for shell or equipment issues, though a plumber handles buried line leaks connecting the pool to its equipment. A household plumbing leak is handled entirely differently. Diagnosing which one you actually have before calling someone saves you from an inspection that finds nothing, because you called the wrong kind of technician for the actual source.

When Leak Detection Covers Both

A comprehensive leak detection visit checks both household supply lines and buried pool plumbing using the same acoustic and thermal equipment, since both are ultimately water lines that can be traced the same way. This means a single visit can often rule in or rule out both possibilities rather than requiring two separate service calls.

Seasonal Factors Worth Ruling Out First

Before assuming a leak, consider whether recent weather has been unusually hot and dry, which genuinely increases evaporation rate on its own, or whether the pool has had unusually heavy use, more splash-out from swimming activity is a normal, non-leak explanation for a faster-than-usual drop. The bucket test accounts for evaporation but not splash-out, so factor that in if the pool's seen a lot of activity.

What Happens If You Ignore A Confirmed Leak

A confirmed pool leak that goes unaddressed doesn't just waste water and money on refills, it can also affect the pool's chemical balance, since fresh fill water dilutes chemical treatment, and in some cases, can affect the structural stability of the surrounding pool deck if a leak is saturating the ground beneath it over an extended period.

Why Acting Early Makes The Fix Simpler

A pool or plumbing leak caught early is almost always a more straightforward repair than one that's been running for months. Early on, a leak is typically isolated to a single fitting or joint. Left unaddressed, that same small failure can erode surrounding material or, in the case of a buried line, saturate enough surrounding soil to complicate access during the eventual repair. The bucket test costs nothing and takes a day, making it one of the easiest home maintenance habits to build into a regular routine, especially heading into a season with more pool use.

The Bottom Line

Pool ownership adds a genuine extra variable to diagnosing water loss, but it doesn't have to mean guessing between two possible sources or paying for two separate inspections. The bucket test narrows things down for free in a day, and a comprehensive leak detection visit can check both household and pool plumbing using the same equipment in a single appointment. Rather than assuming the worst about either your pool or your house, a methodical check of both gets you a real answer faster than most homeowners expect.

Frequently Asked

How much pool water loss is considered normal from evaporation alone?

It varies by climate and season, but the bucket test is the most reliable way to separate normal evaporation from an actual leak in your specific situation.

Can a leak detection technician also fix a pool shell crack?

Buried plumbing lines connecting to the pool, yes. A crack in the pool shell or lining itself is typically outside a plumber's scope and would need a pool specialist.

Should I stop using the pool while investigating a suspected leak?

Not necessarily, though avoiding unnecessary auto-fill or top-offs during your investigation period gives you a cleaner read on the actual loss rate, and keeping a simple daily log of the water level makes the eventual conversation with whoever inspects it much more useful, since a documented pattern over several days is more convincing than a single day's reading either way, and it also helps rule out a one-off event like unusually heavy splash-out from a pool party rather than an actual ongoing leak.

Does a leaking pool affect my household water pressure?

Not typically, since pool auto-fill and household plumbing are usually on separate branches, though a severe leak can affect overall water usage and cost regardless.
