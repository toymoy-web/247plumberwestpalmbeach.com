Buying An Older Home In West Palm Beach? Get A Sewer Camera Inspection First

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Last updated October 2026.

A standard home inspection doesn't look inside the sewer line. That matters in West Palm Beach, where many established homes still have original clay or cast iron lines under the slab and yard. A camera inspection before closing shows what the line looks like before it becomes your repair bill.

Why This Matters More For Older Properties

Homes built decades ago often still have their original clay or cast iron sewer lines, materials that are now well past the point where deterioration, root intrusion, and joint failure become common. A newer home built with modern PVC piping carries far less of this risk, but an older property is exactly where a hidden issue is most likely to be waiting.

What The Inspection Actually Involves

A sewer camera, a waterproof camera on a flexible cable, is fed through an existing cleanout or access point and run the length of the line, recording footage as it goes. The camera includes a locator that can pinpoint the surface position of any defect found, which is useful information for both a repair estimate and price negotiation.

What It Can Reveal

Root intrusion, cracked or collapsed sections, bellied pipe (a low spot that collects standing waste), and general deterioration all show up clearly on camera footage. Sometimes the line is in fine condition and the inspection simply provides peace of mind. Other times it reveals a five-figure repair that would otherwise have surfaced as a surprise emergency months after closing.

Why Older West Palm Beach Homes Deserve A Closer Look

Homes built from the 1950s through the late 1970s were often plumbed with cast iron drain lines under the slab, and South Florida's humidity and high water table can speed up their corrosion. Because those lines sit beneath the concrete, a problem can stay invisible until a backup or a damp floor shows up.

Established neighborhoods such as El Cid, Flamingo Park, Grandview Heights, and Old Northwood also have mature trees whose roots can reach older clay and cast iron joints. A camera run shows root intrusion, cracks, sags, and offsets before you close, which is when you still have leverage.

Insurance adds another reason. Florida insurers commonly ask for a four-point inspection on older homes, often at 25 to 30 years of age or more, and the plumbing section looks at material, age, and visible leaks. Polybutylene, heavily corroded galvanized pipe, and plumbing past roughly 50 years old can delay or limit coverage, so know what you're buying before you commit.

Using The Findings In Negotiation

If a camera inspection finds a genuine problem before closing, that's leverage for a price adjustment or a seller-funded repair, handled before you own the property rather than after. Waiting until after you've moved in means the repair cost is entirely yours.

This Applies To Sellers Too

If you're selling an older home, consider getting a camera inspection yourself, before a buyer's inspection surfaces a surprise. It gives you the option to fix a real issue on your own timeline or price the home accordingly, rather than getting caught in a late-stage negotiation.

What A Clean Inspection Actually Tells You

Not every older home has sewer line problems. A clean camera inspection on a decades-old property is entirely possible, particularly if the line was replaced or relined at some point in the home's history. In that case, the inspection still has value: it converts an unknown into documented peace of mind, which matters just as much in a purchase decision as finding a problem does.

How This Fits Into Your Overall Inspection Timeline

A sewer camera inspection can typically be scheduled quickly once you're under contract, and results are available the same day in most cases. Building it into your inspection period alongside the standard home inspection, rather than treating it as an afterthought, keeps it from becoming a scheduling bottleneck later in the transaction.

What If The Seller Already Has A Recent Inspection Report

If a seller provides a sewer camera inspection report from earlier in the listing process, ask when it was performed and whether anything has changed since. That matters most after recent heavy rain or any plumbing work on the property.

A report that's more than a few months old, or one performed by a company you can't verify, is worth confirming independently. So much rides on that single data point in a real estate transaction.

The Bottom Line

A sewer line is one of the most expensive systems in a home to repair and one of the easiest to overlook during a purchase, since it's completely out of sight and outside the scope of a standard inspection. The cost of a camera inspection is a small fraction of what a surprise sewer line failure costs after closing, and the process itself is quick enough that it rarely holds up a transaction timeline.

Whether you're buying or selling an older property, treating this the same way you'd treat a roof or foundation check, as a routine, expected part of due diligence rather than an optional extra, protects everyone involved in the transaction.

Quick Recap

If you're buying or selling a home built more than a couple of decades ago, build a sewer camera inspection into the transaction timeline the same way you would a roof or foundation check. It's quick, inexpensive relative to what it can reveal, and gives everyone involved a clearer picture before money changes hands. This is true whether the property is a starter home or a larger investment, the underlying sewer infrastructure doesn't care about price point, and neither should the due diligence around it.

Under contract on an older home, or preparing to list one? A sewer camera inspection gives you a clear answer before it becomes someone's surprise.

Frequently Asked

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Most residential inspections take under an hour, including accessing the cleanout and reviewing the footage with you afterward.

Do I get to keep the footage?

Yes, footage and a written summary of findings are provided, which you can share with your realtor, inspector, or attorney as part of the transaction.

Does a clean inspection guarantee no future sewer issues?

No inspection can guarantee future performance, but it does confirm the current condition of the line, which is the most useful information available at the time of purchase.

What if the inspection reveals a problem, does that void the deal?

Not automatically. It typically opens a negotiation about who addresses the repair or how the price reflects it, rather than ending the transaction outright.

Does a Florida four-point inspection include the sewer line?

A four-point inspection typically looks at the visible condition, age, and material of the plumbing. It does not scope the underground sewer line, so a camera inspection fills that gap before you close.
