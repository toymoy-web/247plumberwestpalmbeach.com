Slab Leaks 101: Signs, Causes, And What To Expect

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team

The phrase "slab leak" tends to alarm homeowners more than almost any other plumbing term, and understandably so, it implies a problem hidden under the very foundation of the house. The good news is that a slab leak, while genuinely serious, is also one of the more precisely diagnosable plumbing issues once the right equipment is involved.

What A Slab Leak Actually Is

In slab-on-grade construction, which is standard throughout this area, water supply lines often run directly beneath the concrete foundation rather than through walls or a crawlspace. When one of these lines develops a leak, whether from age, corrosion, or ground shift, the water has nowhere to go but into and under the slab.

The Signs Homeowners Actually Notice

A warm patch on the flooring (usually a sign of a hot water line leak), an unexplained spike in the water bill, the sound of running water audible under the floor with everything off, and in more advanced cases, cracking in tile or drywall from prolonged moisture exposure affecting the foundation.

Why You Shouldn't Wait

A slab leak that's left unaddressed doesn't resolve itself, it continues wasting water and, over time, can begin to affect the structural integrity of the foundation itself if enough water saturates the ground beneath it. The cost and disruption of addressing it early is almost always less than addressing it after months of unnoticed leaking.

How It Gets Found Without Demolition

Modern leak detection uses acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak's exact location before any concrete is touched. This is a meaningful improvement over older approaches that sometimes involved opening a wide search area and hoping to find the source.

Repair Or Reroute: The Real Choice

Once located, there are generally two paths: a direct repair, opening a small, targeted section of slab to fix the pipe in place, or a reroute, which runs a new line above the slab (often through the attic) and abandons the damaged section. Which makes more sense depends on the leak's location and the home's layout, and a good technician will walk through both options with real cost comparisons rather than defaulting to one.

What The Repair Process Actually Looks Like Day-Of

Once a repair path is chosen, the actual work is more contained than most homeowners expect. A direct repair involves opening a targeted section of flooring and slab, exposing the pipe, making the repair, and patching the opening. A reroute avoids the slab entirely, running new line through the attic or along an exterior wall chase. Neither typically takes more than a single day for a straightforward residential case.

Why Multiple Slab Leaks Sometimes Happen Over Time

If a home has copper supply lines that are reaching the end of their expected service life, a slab leak in one spot can sometimes be an early sign that other sections of the same pipe run are approaching similar wear. This is worth discussing with your technician, particularly if a home has already had more than one slab leak, since it may point toward a broader repiping conversation rather than treating each leak as an isolated event.

What Homeowners Often Get Wrong About Timing

A common misconception is that a slab leak needs to be addressed the exact moment it's noticed or the situation is unsalvageable. In reality, most slab leaks develop slowly enough that scheduling a detection visit within a few days of noticing symptoms is entirely reasonable, rather than treating every case as a same-hour emergency. The exception is a leak severe enough to cause visible water pooling or a rapidly climbing bill increase, which does warrant faster action. Understanding this distinction helps homeowners make a calm, informed decision rather than panicking at the first warm spot on the floor.

The Bottom Line

Slab leaks earn their reputation as one of the more unsettling plumbing problems precisely because they're hidden by definition, but the actual process for finding and fixing one is far more straightforward and far less destructive than most homeowners assume before going through it. Modern detection equipment replaces guesswork with precision, and the choice between a direct repair and a reroute gives homeowners real options rather than a single, forced path. The most important step is simply not ignoring the early signs, a warm floor patch, an unexplained bill jump, since a slab leak addressed early is a contained, manageable repair, while one left to run for months risks compounding into a larger, more expensive problem involving both the plumbing and potentially the flooring or foundation around it.

Frequently Asked

Is a slab leak always an emergency?

Not always in the way a burst pipe is, but it should be treated as urgent rather than something to put off, since it continues to waste water and cause potential damage the longer it runs.

Does homeowner's insurance typically cover this?

Many policies cover the cost to access and repair the leak itself, though coverage specifics vary by policy, so it's worth checking with your insurer once a leak is confirmed, ideally with a written diagnosis and repair estimate in hand to make that conversation more concrete.

How long does a typical slab leak take to show up as a symptom?

It varies widely. Some leaks are noticed within days through a bill spike, others go unnoticed for months if the leak is small and the affected area isn't frequently walked on barefoot.
