Slab Leaks In West Palm Beach: Signs, Causes, And What To Expect

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Last updated October 2026.

The phrase "slab leak" alarms West Palm Beach homeowners more than almost any other plumbing term, because it means a problem hidden under the foundation. The good news is that a slab leak, while serious, is one of the more precisely diagnosable plumbing issues once the right equipment is on site.

What A Slab Leak Actually Is

In slab-on-grade construction, which is standard throughout this area, water supply lines often run directly beneath the concrete foundation rather than through walls or a crawlspace. When one of these lines develops a leak, whether from age, corrosion, or ground shift, the water has nowhere to go but into and under the slab.

The Signs Homeowners Actually Notice

The usual signs are a warm patch on the flooring (often a hot water line leak), an unexplained spike in the water bill, and the sound of running water under the floor with everything off. In more advanced cases, tile or drywall may crack from prolonged moisture exposure.

Why You Shouldn't Wait

A slab leak that's left unaddressed doesn't resolve itself, it continues wasting water and, over time, can begin to affect the structural integrity of the foundation itself if enough water saturates the ground beneath it. The cost and disruption of addressing it early is almost always less than addressing it after months of unnoticed leaking.

How It Gets Found Without Demolition

Modern leak detection uses acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak's exact location before any concrete is touched. This is a meaningful improvement over older approaches that sometimes involved opening a wide search area and hoping to find the source.

Repair Or Reroute: The Real Choice

Once located, there are generally two paths. One is a direct repair, which means opening a small, targeted section of slab to fix the pipe in place. The other is a reroute, which runs a new line above the slab (often through the attic) and abandons the damaged section. Which makes more sense depends on the leak's location and the home's layout, and a good technician will walk through both options with real cost comparisons rather than defaulting to one.

What The Repair Process Actually Looks Like Day-Of

Once a repair path is chosen, the actual work is more contained than most homeowners expect. A direct repair involves opening a targeted section of flooring and slab, exposing the pipe, making the repair, and patching the opening. A reroute avoids the slab entirely, running new line through the attic or along an exterior wall chase. Neither typically takes more than a single day for a straightforward residential case.

Why Multiple Slab Leaks Sometimes Happen Over Time

If a home has copper supply lines nearing the end of their expected service life, a slab leak in one spot can be an early sign that other sections of the same run are approaching similar wear. This is worth discussing with your technician, particularly if a home has already had more than one slab leak, since it may point toward a broader repiping conversation rather than treating each leak as an isolated event.

Why Slab Leaks Are A Local Issue Here

Slab-on-grade construction is standard in this part of Florida, so supply lines and drain lines often run under the concrete. Many homes built from the 1950s through the late 1970s also have cast iron drain lines buried beneath the slab, and South Florida's humidity, high water table, and shifting soil can speed up corrosion on those older lines.

That is why a slab leak call in an older West Palm Beach home sometimes involves a drain line as well as the supply. A camera inspection alongside detection shows which one you're dealing with, and homes built before 1980 deserve a closer look than newer construction.

If the leak is on your side of the city meter, a plumber's dated report also supports a leak-credit request. The city's rule has a seven-day repair window, so call Public Utilities early to learn what paperwork they need.

What Homeowners Often Get Wrong About Timing

A common misconception is that a slab leak needs to be addressed the exact moment it's noticed or the situation is unsalvageable. In reality, most slab leaks develop slowly enough that scheduling a detection visit within a few days of noticing symptoms is entirely reasonable, rather than treating every case as a same-hour emergency.

The exception is a leak severe enough to cause visible water pooling or a rapidly climbing bill increase, which does warrant faster action. Understanding this distinction helps homeowners make a calm, informed decision rather than panicking at the first warm spot on the floor.

The Bottom Line

Slab leaks earn their reputation as one of the more unsettling plumbing problems because they're hidden by definition. The process for finding and fixing one is far more straightforward, and far less destructive, than most homeowners assume. Modern detection equipment replaces guesswork with precision, and the choice between a direct repair and a reroute gives homeowners real options rather than a single, forced path.

The most important step is not ignoring the early signs, such as a warm floor patch or an unexplained bill jump. A slab leak addressed early is a contained, manageable repair. One left to run for months can grow into a larger, more expensive problem involving the flooring or foundation around it.

Frequently Asked

Is a slab leak always an emergency?

Not always in the way a burst pipe is. But it should be treated as urgent rather than something to put off, since it continues to waste water and cause potential damage the longer it runs.

Does homeowner's insurance typically cover this?

Many policies cover the cost to access and repair the leak itself, though coverage varies by policy. Check with your insurer once a leak is confirmed, ideally with a written diagnosis and repair estimate in hand.

How long does a typical slab leak take to show up as a symptom?

It varies widely. Some leaks are noticed within days through a bill spike, others go unnoticed for months if the leak is small and the affected area isn't frequently walked on barefoot.

Are slab leaks common in West Palm Beach?

Slab-on-grade construction is standard here, so supply and drain lines often run under the concrete. Older homes with original copper or cast iron lines are the most likely to develop leaks, and humidity plus a high water table add stress over time.
