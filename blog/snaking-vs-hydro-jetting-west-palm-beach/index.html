Snaking Vs. Hydro Jetting In West Palm Beach: Which Does Your Drain Need?

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Last updated October 2026.

Not every clogged drain needs the same fix. In West Palm Beach, where older cast iron lines, mature landscaping, and busy restaurant kitchens each cause a different kind of blockage, choosing between snaking and hydro jetting saves money and repeat visits.

What Snaking Actually Does

A drain snake is a flexible cable, sometimes with a cutting or retrieving head, fed into the pipe to physically break up or pull out whatever is blocking it. It's fast, effective for a single, isolated clog, like a foreign object or a localized hair and soap buildup, and it's the right first move for most simple household clogs.

Where Snaking Falls Short

Snaking punches a path through a blockage rather than removing what's coating the pipe walls around it. For grease buildup that's accumulated over months or years, or for root intrusion, snaking often provides only temporary relief, the clog frequently returns within weeks because the underlying material is still there.

What Hydro Jetting Does Differently

Hydro jetting uses a specialized nozzle to blast high-pressure water in multiple directions as it moves through the pipe, scouring the entire interior surface clean rather than just clearing a center path. It's more effective against grease, scale, and root intrusion precisely because it removes the material rather than pushing past it.

When Jetting Is The Right Call

A drain that clogs again shortly after snaking, a kitchen line with years of grease buildup, or a sewer line with confirmed or suspected root intrusion are all strong candidates for jetting over repeated snaking. It typically costs more upfront but keeps a line clear significantly longer, often resulting in fewer total service calls.

Why A Camera Inspection Often Comes First

Rather than guessing which method fits, a camera inspection shows the actual condition of the pipe, confirming whether grease, roots, or something else is the real cause, and whether the pipe material and condition can safely handle jetting pressure. This avoids paying for the more expensive method when simple snaking would have solved it, and avoids under-treating a line that genuinely needs jetting.

What Clogs Look Like In West Palm Beach Homes

Many older West Palm Beach homes still have original cast iron or clay drain lines, and roots from mature landscaping find their way into the joints. A snake can punch through a root clog, but the roots grow back unless the pipe wall is cleaned and the line is checked. Jetting clears far more of the pipe than a cable can.

Downtown and along the Clematis Street restaurant strip, grease is the usual culprit. It hardens into a coating inside the line, and a snake only opens a small channel through it. A kitchen line with repeat backups usually needs jetting plus a grease management plan.

If a drain backs up more than once in a few months, ask for a camera look before paying for another snaking visit. The camera shows whether you have roots, grease, a sagging section, or a cracked pipe, and that decides which method makes sense.

A Realistic Cost Comparison

Snaking is generally the less expensive option for a single service call. But if it needs repeating every few weeks because the underlying cause was never addressed, the cumulative cost of repeat visits can exceed what a single hydro jetting service would have cost upfront. Thinking in terms of total cost over a year, rather than the price of a single visit, often changes which option actually makes more financial sense.

Maintenance Jetting As A Preventive Option

For lines with a documented history of grease buildup or root intrusion, some property owners choose to schedule jetting on an annual basis as preventive maintenance rather than waiting for the next clog. This is more common in commercial kitchens and properties with known root intrusion issues than in typical residential settings, where addressing an active clog is usually the trigger.

What Homeowners Can Do Between Professional Visits

Simple habits reduce how often either method is needed. Avoid pouring grease down kitchen drains, use drain covers to catch hair before it enters bathroom drains, and run hot water periodically through laundry and kitchen lines to discourage buildup. None of this replaces professional cleaning once a genuine clog develops, but it does stretch the interval between service calls, especially on a line that's already been properly jetted and started clean.

The Bottom Line

Neither snaking nor hydro jetting is inherently the "better" method. They solve different problems. Using the wrong one either wastes money on unnecessary jetting or leaves you calling back in a few weeks after snaking only masked a deeper issue. The most reliable way to make the right call, rather than guessing based on symptoms alone, is a quick camera look at what's actually happening inside the pipe.

That one step turns a decision that's often made by trial and error into one based on an actual diagnosis, which tends to save both money and repeat frustration over time.

Quick Recap

A single, isolated clog usually calls for snaking. A clog that returns within weeks, or a line with known grease or root history, usually calls for jetting. When in doubt, a quick camera look answers the question before you pay for either. Property owners who've dealt with the same recurring clog more than twice are past the point where guessing makes financial sense, a camera inspection at that stage typically pays for itself in avoided repeat visits.

Frequently Asked

Is hydro jetting safe for all pipe types?

Pressure is adjusted based on the pipe's age and material. Older or more fragile pipe is jetted at a lower, safer pressure than modern PVC, which is one reason a camera check beforehand matters.

Can I request jetting even for a simple clog?

You can, but if a camera or simple diagnostic shows it's an isolated, one-time blockage, snaking is usually the more cost-effective choice, and a good technician should tell you that rather than upselling jetting unnecessarily.

How long does a typical hydro jetting service take?

Most residential jetting services are completed in one to two hours, depending on line length and the extent of buildup being cleared.

Do you need to remove any fixtures to access the line?

Usually not. Most homes have an existing cleanout that provides direct access without needing to remove a toilet or other fixture.

Which is better for a main line clog in West Palm Beach?

A first-time, isolated clog often clears with snaking. A main line that backs up repeatedly, or one with a known root or grease history, usually needs jetting after a camera look confirms what's in the pipe.
