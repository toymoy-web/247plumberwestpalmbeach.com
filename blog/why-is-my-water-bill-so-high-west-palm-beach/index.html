Why Is My Water Bill So High in West Palm Beach?

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Last updated October 2026.

If your water bill jumped noticeably from one month to the next and nothing about your household changed, the most likely explanation is a leak somewhere in your system, not a billing error. West Palm Beach's combination of slab-on-grade construction and, in older neighborhoods, aging pipe material makes leaks a genuinely common cause of unexplained bill increases here.

Start With The Meter

Before assuming the worst, check your water meter with every fixture in the house turned off. If the dial is still moving, or the leak indicator (a small triangular or star-shaped dial on most meters) is spinning, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures. If the meter is still, the increase might trace to something else, like an irrigation system running longer than expected or a new appliance.

According to West Palm Beach's city code, Section 90-12, a qualifying leak on your side of the meter may be eligible for a billing adjustment if a certified plumber repairs it within seven days. Ask the utility how to apply, and keep your plumber's written findings and invoice.

The Most Common Culprits

In our experience with West Palm Beach homes, three sources account for most unexplained bill jumps. They are a running toilet flapper that's silently leaking into the bowl, a slab leak under the concrete foundation, and an irrigation or pool line leak in the yard. A running toilet is usually the cheapest and fastest to fix. A slab leak is the one that requires professional detection equipment, since it's invisible by definition.

Why Slab Leaks Are Common Here

Nearly every home in this area is built slab-on-grade, meaning supply lines run directly under the concrete foundation with no crawlspace to inspect. Combine that with decades-old copper or galvanized lines in many established neighborhoods, and slab leaks show up here more often than in regions with basement or crawlspace plumbing.

Don't Forget The Pool

If you have a pool, its equipment pad and buried lines are a genuine, often-overlooked source of water loss. A cracked pipe at the equipment pad or a leak in a buried pool line can run for weeks before anyone notices the pool level dropping, especially if it's topped off automatically.

What A Professional Check Actually Involves

A leak detection visit starts by isolating sections of your plumbing to narrow down which branch is losing water, then uses acoustic or thermal equipment to pinpoint the exact location without guessing. That targeted approach means any necessary repair is planned before anything gets opened up, not discovered by trial and error.

What To Do While You're Waiting For A Diagnosis

If you suspect a leak but haven't scheduled an inspection yet, note the meter reading and check it again a day later with usage as normal. That gives you a rough sense of the daily loss rate. That can help a technician gauge severity before they even arrive. Avoid the temptation to start opening walls or digging in the yard yourself looking for the source. Without detection equipment, this usually just adds repair cost without finding anything.

What The City Of West Palm Beach Leak Credit Covers

If you're on city water, West Palm Beach has a written leak-credit rule in Section 90-12 of the city code. It applies to leaks on your side of the meter, and the repair has to be made by a certified plumber within seven days of finding the leak or receiving the high bill.

To ask for the adjustment, you send Public Utilities Customer Service a copy of the contested bill, the plumber's repair invoice, and the city's leak adjustment form, all within 30 days of the bill date. If it's approved, the city credits the water and sewer charges above your average bill for one billing cycle, and the credit can be used once in any 36-month period.

That seven-day window is the reason a fast diagnosis matters. A written leak detection report gives you the dated paperwork the city asks for. Rules like these can change, so confirm the current terms with Public Utilities before you count on a credit, and know that homes served by a different utility follow that utility's own policy.

How Insurance Sometimes Factors In

Depending on your homeowner's policy, the cost to access and repair a confirmed leak, particularly a slab leak, may be partially covered, though coverage for the water damage itself and coverage for the repair are often treated differently. It's worth having a clear diagnosis and repair estimate in hand before contacting your insurer, since a vague description of "a high water bill" is much harder to process than a documented leak location and repair scope.

A Realistic Timeline From First Call To Resolution

Most leak detection visits are completed within a single appointment, typically an hour or two depending on the complexity of your plumbing layout and how many branches need to be isolated and checked. If the leak is found in an easily accessible location, the repair itself can sometimes happen the same visit. A slab leak or a leak requiring a section of pipe reroute may need a short follow-up appointment once the repair approach is confirmed and any necessary materials are on hand.

Either way, you should have a clear answer about the source of your water loss well before the day is over. That is a meaningful improvement over the weeks of uncertainty that come with just watching your water bill and hoping it resolves itself.

The Bottom Line

An unexplained water bill increase is frustrating precisely because the cause is invisible by nature, but it's also one of the more reliably diagnosable plumbing problems once the right approach is used.

Checking the meter yourself costs nothing and takes five minutes. It immediately tells you whether you're dealing with a genuine leak or something else entirely, like a longer irrigation cycle or a new appliance. From there, professional detection equipment does the rest, narrowing an invisible problem down to a specific, addressable location rather than leaving you to guess or wait it out.

The sooner that diagnosis happens, the less water gets wasted and the less opportunity a slab leak or supply line failure has to cause secondary damage. A bill that suddenly doesn't match your household's normal pattern is worth taking seriously the same month it happens, not several billing cycles later.

Frequently Asked

How much water loss actually shows up on a bill?

Even a slow leak, one you'd never notice by looking, can waste hundreds of gallons a month, which is often enough to visibly move the needle on a monthly bill.

Should I call my water utility or a plumber first?

Most utilities can confirm whether your meter itself is moving with everything off, but they won't locate or fix the leak. A plumber handles the actual diagnosis and repair.

Is it possible the increase isn't a leak at all?

Yes. A new appliance, a longer irrigation schedule, or even a guest staying longer than usual can all move the needle. Checking the meter with everything off is still the fastest way to rule leak sources in or out before assuming the worst.

Does West Palm Beach credit a high water bill caused by a leak?

The city's leak-credit rule can credit water and sewer charges above your average bill for one billing cycle when the leak is on your side of the meter and a certified plumber repairs it within seven days. Confirm the current terms with Public Utilities Customer Service before you file.
