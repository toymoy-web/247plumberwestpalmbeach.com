Emergency Services

Plumbing Emergencies We Handle 24/7

Some jobs cannot wait for the next business day. Our emergency line is open around the clock for leak detection, broken water lines, sewer backups, clogged drains, water heater failures, gas line problems, and water damage or mitigation. Each service page explains what to do while you wait and what we do when we arrive.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 or your gas utility from outside before you call us. For anything else, shut off the main water valve if you can and call (561) 448-8545.

Choosing A Service

Which Service Fits Your Problem?

A high water bill, a meter that spins with everything off, or a warm spot on the floor usually points to leak detection or slab leak repair. Slow drains in several fixtures suggest a main line problem, which hydro jetting or a sewer camera inspection can sort out. No hot water, a rumbling tank, or water around the heater calls for water heater repair, and an aging unit may be better replaced through water heater installation.

Older homes in the historic districts may need repiping when leaks keep returning. Homes on septic need septic tank pumping, and households with taste or odor complaints should look at water filtration before a softener.

Pricing And Process

How Pricing Works

Every service page lists a typical range, but your price comes from an inspection of your actual situation. We quote a flat price before any repair work begins, so you approve the cost first and there are no hourly surprises on the invoice.

We recommend repair when it makes sense and replacement only when it does not. If a service would not help your situation, such as a softener when your water test shows low mineral content, we will tell you.

Homes And Businesses

Residential And Commercial Work

These services cover single-family homes, condominiums, and commercial properties such as restaurants, offices, and retail spaces. Commercial plumbing includes after-hours emergency dispatch and coordination with property managers and tenants.

Many jobs on this page can be avoided or caught early. Flushing a tank water heater every year, putting a camera in an older sewer line before a purchase, and locating your main shutoff valve before an emergency all cost little compared with a failure. Our plumbing blog has local guides on each of these topics.

Before You Call

What To Have Ready

A few details help the dispatcher send the right technician. Know your address, what you are seeing or hearing, and whether you have shut off the water. Mention the age of the home or building and the type of plumbing if you know it, and note any gate codes or building management contacts for condominiums.
