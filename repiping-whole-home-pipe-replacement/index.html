FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if I have polybutylene pipe?

It's typically gray plastic pipe, often visible where it enters the water heater. If you're unsure, we can confirm during an inspection.

Is repiping really necessary, or can I keep patching leaks?

If leaks are recurring in different spots, patching becomes more expensive over time than a single repipe that ends the pattern.

How much of my walls will need to be opened?

We plan access points to minimize this, and most repipes are completed with far less drywall work than homeowners expect.

Can this be done in stages instead of all at once?

In some cases, yes, we can phase a repipe by section, though a single continuous project is usually more cost-effective overall.

Why do I keep getting new leaks in different spots even after fixing the last one?

When multiple unrelated leaks show up over a short period, it usually means the pipe material itself, often aging galvanized steel or polybutylene, is failing throughout the house, not just at the one spot you patched.

Scope Of Service

What's Included

A full repipe is planned by mapping every fixture's connection point and identifying the most efficient access route, typically through attic space, wall chases, or under-slab tunneling where a slab home requires it. We use PEX or copper depending on your preference and budget; PEX offers lower material cost and freeze resistance, while copper offers a longer proven track record and higher heat tolerance. Fixtures are reconnected and pressure-tested at every point before we close any opened walls, with all work performed and permitted to the Florida Building Code, Plumbing (based on the International Plumbing Code, or IPC).

Local Notes

Serving West Palm Beach & Palm Beach County

Homes built in certain construction booms in this region are documented to have used polybutylene piping, a material with a well-known, widely reported failure pattern that makes a full repipe the more sensible long-term fix rather than repeated patch repairs.

After The Job

What To Expect Next

Once a full repipe is complete, the new system generally requires no special maintenance beyond normal water heater care, since modern PEX and copper don't share the corrosion and failure patterns of the older galvanized or polybutylene material being replaced. We'll walk through where the new shutoff valves are located before we finish, since that layout sometimes changes during a repipe.

Every new line is pressure-tested before any access points are closed up, so any connection issue is caught while the walls are still open rather than after the job is finished.

Related Services

Often Booked Together

A repipe project is often triggered by findings from a leak detection visit or a pattern of repeat failures on the same water line. Once interior plumbing is replaced, it's a natural point to also address the exterior service line if it hasn't been done already.
