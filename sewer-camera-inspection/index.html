Sewer Camera Inspection in West Palm Beach, FL

Written by the West Palm Beach 24/7 Plumbing team. Last updated October 2026.

A sewer camera inspection shows the actual, current condition of the line, root intrusion, cracks, bellies, or blockages, rather than guessing based on symptoms alone. This is useful both for diagnosing a recurring problem and for confirming a line's condition before a home purchase. It's also useful evidence to have on hand before authorizing a larger repair, since it removes the guesswork from what's actually being paid for.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is a sewer camera inspection worth it before buying a home?

Yes, it's one of the only ways to see the actual condition of a line that's otherwise completely hidden, and it's a common contingency request for older homes.

Can you tell me exactly where a crack or root intrusion is?

Yes, camera inspections include location tracking so we can pinpoint the defect's position from the surface if needed.

Do I need a cleanout for this, or can you access the line another way?

An existing cleanout is easiest, but we can typically find another entry point if one isn't already installed.

Can I get a copy of the video footage?

Yes, footage and a written summary of findings are provided as part of the service.

Scope Of Service

What's Included

The camera unit is self-leveling and includes a built-in locator that lets us mark the exact surface position of any defect it finds. That is useful both for planning a repair and for showing a home buyer or seller precisely what condition the line is in. Inspections are recorded start to finish, and we provide the footage along with a written summary rather than just a verbal description of what we saw.

Local Notes

Serving West Palm Beach & Palm Beach County

With so much of West Palm Beach's residential inventory dating to earlier decades, a pre-purchase camera inspection has become a routine part of due diligence for buyers here, the same way a roof or foundation inspection would be.

West Palm Beach Neighborhoods

Camera Inspections Before You Buy

Camera inspections are routine for buyers in the historic districts, where the sewer lateral under a 1920s home in El Cid, Flamingo Park, or Old Northwood may be clay or cast iron of unknown condition. We also inspect after repeated backups on streets with large shade trees, where roots are the usual cause. The footage and a short written summary go to you, so it can be used in a purchase negotiation or to plan a repair.

After The Job

What To Expect Next

If the inspection found no significant issues, no follow-up is needed until symptoms actually appear again. If it identified developing root intrusion or a minor crack not yet causing problems, we'll recommend a re-inspection interval based on severity rather than an immediate repair, so you're not paying for work the line doesn't need yet.

We provide the actual recorded footage, not just a verbal summary, so you or a home inspector, contractor, or future buyer can review the line's condition independently of our findings.

Related Services

Often Booked Together

Once an inspection identifies the actual issue, the right next step depends on what's found: sewer line repair for a structural problem, or hydro jetting if it's grease or root buildup rather than damage. We'll tell you plainly which one your footage actually calls for.
