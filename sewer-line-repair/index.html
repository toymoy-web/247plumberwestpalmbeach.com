FAQs

Common Questions

Do you always recommend full replacement?

No. A camera inspection often shows the damage is localized enough for a spot repair or trenchless lining, which costs significantly less.

Why do my drains keep backing up in the same spot?

In older neighborhoods here, that's frequently root intrusion at a cracked joint that keeps regrowing after simple clearing.

Is a permit required for this repair in West Palm Beach?

Yes, sewer line repair work falls under Florida Building Code and requires city inspection sign-off, which we handle as part of the job.

How long does a typical sewer line repair take?

A spot repair is often completed in a single day. A full replacement can take one to three days depending on access and length.

Scope Of Service

What's Included

Repair options fall into three groups. A localized spot repair excavates and replaces or lines only the damaged section. A full trenchless pipe lining rehabilitates the entire run without digging it up. Full excavation and replacement is for lines with extensive, distributed damage.

Which option applies is determined by camera inspection findings, not assumed upfront. Permit filing and the required city inspection are included as part of any sewer line repair job in West Palm Beach, performed to the Florida Building Code for plumbing (based on the International Plumbing Code, or IPC).

Local Notes

Serving West Palm Beach & Palm Beach County

Palm Beach County's older residential blocks were largely built with clay and cast iron sewer lines that are now decades past their original service life. The region's large, established trees make root intrusion at pipe joints a frequent finding rather than a rare one.

West Palm Beach Neighborhoods

Sewer Line Problems In Historic West Palm Beach

El Cid, Flamingo Park, Grandview Heights, and Old Northwood were built in the 1920s and 1930s, and many sewer laterals there are older than the trees growing over them. When a line cracks or a joint separates, roots follow within a season or two. We map the damage with a camera first and then decide between a spot repair, lining, or full replacement, with the city inspection handled as part of the job.

After The Job

What To Expect Next

After a spot repair or trenchless lining, we recommend a follow-up camera pass at the one-year mark, particularly for lines with a history of root intrusion. It lets us catch any regrowth at the repair joint early rather than waiting for another backup. Full replacements generally don't need this same monitoring.

Repairs are camera-verified before we consider the job complete, and we'll provide the before-and-after footage so you can see the actual condition of the line, not just take our word for it.
