FAQs

Common Questions

How do I know if my sump pump has failed?

The most obvious sign is water where it shouldn't be, but a pump that's running constantly or never running during rain is also a warning sign worth checking before it fails completely.

Is battery backup really necessary here?

If your property has flooded during a power outage before, or is prone to standing water, backup power is worth the added cost for storm-season peace of mind.

Can this be installed if I've never had a sump pump before?

Yes, we install new systems in properties without one, sized to the actual water risk on site.

How often should an existing sump pump be tested?

We recommend testing before hurricane season each year, in addition to addressing it immediately if you notice any of the warning signs above.

Why does water keep pooling in the same spot every time it rains heavily?

That's usually a drainage or grading issue that a sump pump is specifically designed to manage, either because one isn't installed there yet or because an existing one isn't functioning correctly.

Scope Of Service

What's Included

Repairs most commonly involve the float switch, the component that senses rising water and triggers the pump, since it fails more often than the motor itself. New installations include sizing the pump to your pit's capacity and the property's actual water intrusion risk, plus an optional battery backup system that keeps the pump running through a power outage. That matters most during the exact storms most likely to cause flooding in the first place.

Local Notes

Serving West Palm Beach & Palm Beach County

West Palm Beach's flat terrain and high water table mean stormwater has fewer places to drain naturally during heavy rain events. That puts real, recurring demand on sump pump systems during the region's wet season and hurricane season alike.

West Palm Beach Neighborhoods

Sump Pumps And Low-Lying Properties Here

True sump pumps are uncommon in West Palm Beach because most homes are built at grade with no basement. The calls we get are for low-lying lots, garages, and ground-floor spaces that take on water during heavy rain. Properties near the Lake Worth Lagoon and the Intracoastal sit in a high-water-table area, and older streets around Flamingo Park and Grandview Heights can pond when storm drains are overwhelmed. We size a pump to the real water source on site instead of a generic basement model.

After The Job

What To Expect Next

We recommend testing the pump manually before hurricane season each year by pouring water into the pit and confirming it activates and drains properly, in addition to whatever we already checked during service. If a battery backup was installed, that battery typically needs replacement every few years to stay reliable.

New and repaired systems are tested by simulating a rising water level in the pit, confirming the float switch triggers and the pump clears the water as expected before we call the job done.

Codes And Standards

Work Done To Code

Pit, discharge, and check valve work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), which includes provisions for sumps and pumping systems. We size each pump to the pit and follow the manufacturer's installation guidelines for the model we install.
