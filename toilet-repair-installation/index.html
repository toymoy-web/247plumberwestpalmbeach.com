Emergency Toilet Repair In West Palm Beach, 24/7

A toilet that is overflowing, leaking at the base, or will not stop running cannot wait. Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise to stop the water, then call our 24/7 line for a technician.

If several fixtures are backing up together, the problem is likely further down the line than the toilet itself. We check the flange, the trap, and the main line during the visit.

Common Signs

When To Call Us

Running Constantly

Usually a worn flapper or fill valve letting water leak into the bowl.

Rocking Or Leaking At The Base

Often a failed wax ring or a cracked flange underneath.

Weak Or Incomplete Flush

Can point to a clogged trap way, a low water level, or a worn flush valve.

Frequent Clogging

Older low-flow models sometimes lack the flush force newer designs use.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my toilet run for a few seconds every hour even when unused?

That's typically a slow flapper leak letting water seep from the tank into the bowl, triggering the fill valve periodically.

Is a wobbling toilet a serious problem?

It can be. A rocking toilet often means the wax ring seal is broken, which can let water leak onto the subfloor over time.

Can you install a new toilet the same day?

Yes, standard installations are typically completed same-day when the flange and rough-in are in normal condition.

Can you match a specific bowl height or style?

Yes, we install standard, comfort-height, and various bowl styles based on your preference.

Scope Of Service

What's Included

Common internal repairs include flapper replacement, fill valve replacement, and flush valve seal replacement, all of which address the vast majority of running-toilet and weak-flush complaints. For installations, we check the flange condition and rough-in measurement before setting the new unit, since a damaged flange left unaddressed is one of the most common causes of a toilet leaking again within months of installation.

Local Notes

Serving West Palm Beach & Palm Beach County

Older West Palm Beach homes sometimes still have original flanges and rough-in dimensions that don't match modern standard toilets. That is something we check before quoting a replacement so there are no surprises mid-installation.

West Palm Beach Neighborhoods

Toilet Problems In Older West Palm Beach Homes

Toilet problems in older homes often start below the floor instead of in the bowl. In the 1920s homes of El Cid, Grandview Heights, and Flamingo Park, original flanges and drain stubs can be corroded or misaligned. That is why we check the flange before setting any new toilet. In downtown condominiums the cause is usually a shared stack, so a repeat clog may need building coordination as much as a plumber.

After The Job

What To Expect Next

After a repair or installation, there's generally no ongoing maintenance needed beyond normal use. If you notice any change in flush performance over time, check the water level in the tank, since that's often the earliest sign a component is starting to wear.

Every repair or installation is tested with multiple flush cycles before we leave, checking for leaks at the base, the supply connection, and the tank itself.

Codes And Standards

Work Done To Code

Replacement toilets must meet the federal maximum of 1.6 gallons per flush, and EPA WaterSense models use 1.28 gallons per flush or less. Installation follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).
