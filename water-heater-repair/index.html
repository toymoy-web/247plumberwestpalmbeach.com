A water heater leaking onto the floor, a tank rumbling hard, or no hot water on a cold morning needs attention fast. Call our 24/7 line and we send a technician to diagnose the problem on the first visit.

If the tank is leaking, shut off the cold water supply to the heater and set the power or gas control to off. Then call us, and we will tell you whether a repair makes sense or a replacement is the safer choice.

Common Signs

When To Call Us

No Hot Water At All

Usually a failed heating element or, on gas units, a pilot or ignition issue.

Lukewarm Water

Often a thermostat problem or sediment insulating the heating element.

Puddle Under The Tank

A tank leak from corrosion typically means repair won't hold and replacement is safer.

Popping Or Rumbling Noises

Sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank rumbling as it heats, common when sediment has built up.

FAQs

Common Questions

Is it cheaper to repair or replace my water heater?

If the tank itself hasn't started leaking, repair is almost always cheaper, and we'll tell you honestly which situation you're in.

Why does my water heater need flushing every year?

Minerals in the water settle out as sediment in the tank, and that sediment shortens the unit's life if it's never cleared. How fast it builds depends on your water and usage.

Can you fix a tankless unit, or only tanks?

We service both tank and tankless systems from all major brands.

Do you carry parts for most brands on the truck?

Common parts for major brands are typically stocked, which is part of how most repairs are completed in a single visit.

Scope Of Service

What's Included

Common repairs include heating element replacement, thermostat replacement, thermocouple or igniter repair on gas units, and tank flushing to clear sediment buildup that's insulating the heating element from the water. We carry commonly needed parts for major brands on the truck, which is why most repairs are completed in a single visit rather than requiring a return trip to source a part.

Local Notes

Serving West Palm Beach & Palm Beach County

Mineral sediment is one of the most common reasons water heater components fail early, and heating element and sediment-related failures are routine calls here. This is also why we check sediment buildup as a standard part of any repair visit, even when the reported symptom points to a different component.

West Palm Beach Neighborhoods

Water Heater Repairs In West Palm Beach Homes

Water heaters in historic districts like El Cid, Flamingo Park, and Old Northwood are often installed in closets, garages, or utility rooms with limited clearance, and sediment shortens their life wherever they sit. Condominium towers along Flagler Drive add another case, where a tank leak in one unit can reach neighbors below. We check venting, the drain pan, and shutoff access during every repair so a small fix does not leave a bigger risk behind.

After The Job

What To Expect Next

After a repair, we recommend an annual flush to clear the sediment that builds up over time. That is one of the biggest factors in whether a repaired unit lasts its full remaining service life or fails again sooner. We can note this on a reminder if you'd like.

Repairs are tested under normal operating conditions, running the unit through a full heating cycle, before we consider the job done, not just visually inspected after the part is swapped.

Codes And Standards

Work Done To Code

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC), requires relief valve protection and a safe discharge on storage water heaters. We check the temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge line during a repair, and we follow the manufacturer's installation guidelines.
